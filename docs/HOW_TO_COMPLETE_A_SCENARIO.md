# How to Complete a Scenario — DM Structure Guide

Every scenario in the tabletop app is built to be **completable in roughly 6–8 turns (under 60 minutes)**.
This document explains the two structural layers you work through to win — the **3 story beats** (what phase of the crisis you're in) and the **3 attack-chain stages** (the hidden layers of the attack you must neutralize) — plus the exact winning conditions.

---

## The short version

You win by doing **all three of these**:

1. **Clear the 3 story beats in order** — respond to the public, shut down the fraud/attack, then eradicate + recover.
2. **Contain all 3 attack-chain stages** — find how they got in, how it spread, and what they took; then neutralize each one.
3. **Hit all 5 win-condition thresholds** — trust, regulator confidence, containment, eradication, and recovery all above their target lines.

The DM tracks all of this for you. It reveals chain stages as you investigate, advances beats as you resolve them, and updates your metrics each turn. You don't have to memorize the numbers — just keep making competent, coordinated decisions and the DM moves you forward.

---

## Layer 1 — The 3 story beats (your path through the crisis)

The scenario is an ordered arc of **3 beats**. You're always told which beat you're in. You move to the next beat when your actions genuinely resolve the current one.

Using the **Deepfake CEO** example (the pattern is the same for all scenarios):

| Beat | Phase | What resolves it |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Public response** | Get ahead of the story | Confirm the video is fake, show the CEO is real & reachable, give members one clear instruction (e.g. "don't act on calls/texts, call us"), brief the frontline so every answer matches. |
| **2 — Fraud & takedown** | Drive takedowns + shut down the attack | Take the video down on platforms, find who planted it (compromised channel, amplifier network), shut down the fraud crew, give the regulator the facts & timeline. |
| **3 — Recover** | Eradicate root cause + rebuild | Close & harden the compromised channel, confirm the attack is fully shut out, make victims whole, help law enforcement, rebuild member & board confidence. |

**Pacing rule:** the DM is told to compress this so a strong session finishes all 3 beats in ~6–8 turns. A decisive action can resolve **more than one thing at once** — e.g. a strong public statement can contain a stage AND recover trust in a single turn.

---

## Layer 2 — The 3 attack-chain stages (what you must neutralize)

Beneath the story, each scenario has a hidden **attack chain of 3 stages**. Your investigation reveals them gradually; you must **contain** each one to win.

Using Deepfake CEO:

| Stage | Name | What it is / how you contain it |
|---|---|---|
| **hook** | *How they got in* | The attack was planted through the marketing agency's **compromised social account**. Contain it by investigating & closing that channel. |
| **spread** | *How it spread* | A coordinated **amplifier network** (bot accounts, seeded shares, a cut-down viral clip). Contain it by takedowns + mapping the network. |
| **take** | *What they took* | A **fraud wave** harvesting member funds behind the panic. Contain it by freezing accounts, fraud monitoring, protecting victims. |

The DM reveals a stage when your investigation plausibly uncovers it, then marks it **contained** when you neutralize it. You don't get told the chain upfront — you discover it by doing the right investigative work (looking at logs, tracing the seed, working with platforms, etc.).

---

## Layer 3 — The 5 win conditions (your scoreboard)

To actually win, all five of these must be met **at the same time**:

| Metric | Target (Deepfake CEO) | What drives it up |
|---|---|---|
| **public_trust** | ≥ 60 | Clear, credible public response; CEO visibility; protecting members. |
| **regulator_confidence** | ≥ 55 | Briefing the regulator, delivering facts & a timeline, cooperation. |
| **containment** | ≥ 80 | Takedowns, shutting down the spread. |
| **eradication** | ≥ 70 | Removing the root cause, hardening the channel. |
| **recovery** | ≥ 60 | Making victims whole, restoring operations & confidence. |

> Note: thresholds vary per scenario, but the pattern (trust + regulator + containment + eradication + recovery) is consistent.

---

## How a session actually ends

The game ends in **one of these three ways** — never by a clock, and never by a counter running up:

1. **WIN** — all 5 win conditions met (goal achieved). The DM narrates the resolution.
2. **LOSS (narrative collapse)** — if `public_trust` AND `regulator_confidence` **both** drop to ≤20 and stay there for **2 consecutive turns**, the story ends as a narrated collapse (members lose faith, regulator escalates, board wavers). This is a *narrative* ending, not a number hitting 100.
3. **MANUAL END** — the group chooses to wrap up and the DM produces the closing report.

The 60-minute timer is **only a pace guide** — when it hits zero you get a gentle "recommended wrap-up" note, but the game doesn't force-stop. You finish when you reach a real resolution.

---

## A winning playbook (applies to all scenarios)

1. **Turn 1–2: Get ahead of the story.** Go public with a credible, coordinated response. Address the frontline so everyone says the same thing. A strong start makes beat 1 resolve fast and makes beats 2–3 calmer.
2. **Turn 3–5: Shut it down.** Work takedowns, trace the source (logs, compromised channels), engage the regulator with facts and a timeline, and freeze the fraud. Keep doing coordinated multi-department turns (e.g. "comms issues a statement AND the fraud team freezes accounts AND legal contacts the regulator").
3. **Turn 6–7: Eradicate and recover.** Close and harden the compromised channel, make victims whole, help law enforcement, and rebuild confidence. Don't stop at containment — you need eradication and recovery to actually win.
4. **Do decisive, combined actions.** One strong turn can resolve a beat and a chain stage at once. That's how you finish in ~6–8 turns.

---

## If things go badly

Don't panic on a single bad metric — **one low stat does not end the game**. Only a sustained failure (both trust AND regulator in the danger zone for consecutive turns) triggers the loss, and even then you can fight back until the collapse holds. Keep making sensible moves and the DM gives you room to recover.
